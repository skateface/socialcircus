Baby shower games have a reputation problem, and it's deserved — melting candy bars in diapers and guessing the circumference of a belly with string have been the default for decades, and most guests play along out of politeness, not enjoyment. None of that is required. A shower game just needs to do one job: give a room full of people who may not know each other well something to do together for ten minutes. Here's a list that actually does that, organized by how much space and energy you've got.

## Guessing and prediction games (low setup, any group size)

These work as soon as guests arrive and double as a way to get people talking before the main event starts.

1. **Baby Predictions Cards** — print small cards asking guests to guess the baby's weight, birth date, and which parent they'll resemble more. Collect them in a jar and read the funniest ones out loud later, or save them to compare against reality after the birth.
2. **Who Said It?** — before the shower, text the parent-to-be a handful of quotes from their pregnancy ("I will never eat pickles again") and mix in some made-up ones. Guests guess which quotes are real.
3. **Price Is Right: Baby Edition** — guess the price of 5-6 baby items (a stroller, a pack of diapers, a onesie). Closest guess without going over wins, same rules as the show.
4. **The Wishes for Baby Jar** — guests write one wish or piece of advice for the baby on a card and drop it in a jar. Not really a "game" with a winner, but it's consistently the activity parents say they keep afterward.

## Active games (best for groups of 10+ with some open space)

5. **Diaper Raffle** — guests bring a pack of diapers as their "raffle ticket" for a prize drawing. It's less a game than a clever way to stock the nursery, but it reliably gets everyone involved in the first ten minutes.
6. **Baby Bottle Chugging Relay** — fill baby bottles with juice or water, teams race to "chug" through the nipple. Gets loud and competitive fast — save it for a crowd that's already warmed up.
7. **Diaper Changing Relay (on a doll)** — teams race to diaper a baby doll correctly, blindfolded or with one hand tied, for extra chaos. Consistently the biggest laugh-getter on this list.
8. **Don't Say "Baby"** — give every guest a clothespin or ribbon at the start. Anyone caught saying "baby" during the party has their pin taken by whoever catches them. Whoever has the most pins at the end wins. Runs in the background the entire shower with zero extra setup.

## Creative and keepsake games (best for smaller, seated groups)

9. **Advice & Wishes Book** — similar to the wishes jar, but in a bound book guests write directly into, often alongside a photo station. Give it to the parents as a keepsake instead of a card box of individual notes.
10. **Onesie Decorating Station** — set out plain onesies and fabric markers, let guests decorate one each for the baby's wardrobe. Works as an activity people drift to throughout the party rather than a timed game.
11. **Baby Bingo (for the shower itself)** — print bingo cards with squares like "someone cries," "a gift gets opened before its bow is off," or "someone brings up their own birth story." Guests mark squares as the party unfolds — it turns the whole shower into the game, no separate time slot needed.
12. **Alphabet Baby Names** — go around the room, each guest names a baby name starting with the next letter of the alphabet. Simple, fast, and works for literally any group size without materials.

## How to actually run shower games without losing the room

1. **Pick games based on who's in the room, not what's trendy.** A shower full of coworkers who just met leans toward low-pressure guessing games. A shower full of close friends and family can handle the loud, competitive ones.
2. **Don't make games mandatory.** Run them in a cluster near one side of the room instead of stopping all conversation to announce "game time." People who want in will notice and join; people who'd rather keep talking can.
3. **Stagger games around food, not back to back.** One game as people arrive, a second after everyone's eaten, and stop there. Shower games lose their charm fast once they start feeling like homework.
4. **Always have a prize that costs nothing to source** — a candle, a small plant, "first pick of the gift bag" — so you're not scrambling to buy prizes on top of everything else.
5. **Let the parent-to-be opt out of playing.** They're usually busy opening gifts, greeting guests, and being the center of attention — the games are for everyone else to bond over, not one more thing on their plate.
