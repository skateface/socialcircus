An ugly sweater party has a built-in gimmick that does half the hosting for you — everyone shows up already dressed for a bit. The part that actually takes planning is what happens after people get through the door: how the contest works, who judges it, and what fills the room before and after so the sweaters aren't the only thing going on. Get those three right and the party runs itself.

## 1. Set contest categories before you set a date

A single "best sweater" award sounds simple, but it almost always turns into a popularity contest where the loudest or most-liked guest wins regardless of their actual sweater. Multiple categories fix that and give more people a reason to dress up:

- **Ugliest** — the one that's genuinely hard to look at, in the best way.
- **Funniest** — a pun, a reference, or a visual joke.
- **Most effort / DIY** — rewards someone who built or decorated their own, even if the result is rough.
- **Best thrifted find** — for the person who found an actual vintage monstrosity instead of buying a new novelty one.

Four categories for a group of 10-15 is plenty. Past 20 guests, add a "crowd favorite" category decided by applause or a show of hands, so the room gets a say without it swallowing every other category.

## 2. Decide how judging works before anyone puts a sweater on

Judging drama kills ugly sweater parties faster than anything else. Pick one method in advance and announce it on the invite, not on the night:

1. **Judge panel.** Pick 2-3 people who aren't entering — a non-competing couple, whoever's driving, or just ask for volunteers at the door. A panel is faster and less political than a full room vote.
2. **Secret ballot.** Hand out slips or use a shared phone poll; guests vote by sweater number, not by name, so nobody's voting based on friendship. Works well for groups over 15 where a live show-of-hands gets chaotic.
3. **Applause-o-meter.** Line everyone up, clap for each sweater, and the host eyeballs the loudest response per category. Fastest option, best for smaller, louder groups who don't care about precision.

Whichever you pick, number the sweaters (a sticky note with a number works) so voting doesn't turn into a debate about who's wearing what.

## 3. Time the contest so it's a highlight, not an interruption

The contest shouldn't happen the moment the first guest arrives, and it shouldn't get pushed so late that half the room has already left. For a 2.5-3 hour party:

- **0:00-0:30** — Arrival, drinks, mingling. Let people show off their sweaters informally before anything's official.
- **0:30-1:00** — A low-key warm-up game or two while stragglers arrive, so the contest doesn't start with half the room missing.
- **1:00-1:30** — The contest itself: line up, judge or vote, announce winners per category. Keep it moving — long dead air between categories is where energy drops.
- **1:30-2:30** — Open floor: more games, a photo booth moment with the winners, food, music.
- **2:30-3:00** — Wind-down, with the lightest activity of the night so people have a natural exit point.

## 4. Fill the rest of the night with games that match the vibe

The sweaters set a goofy, low-stakes tone — lean into it rather than switching to something serious once the contest ends.

- **A sweater-themed photo booth.** A cheap backdrop, some string lights, and props turns the sweaters into content instead of just an outfit, and gives guests something to do in the lulls.
- **White elephant or a small gift swap**, if your group already does one — it pairs naturally with the sweater theme. If you want the full rundown on running that part, our <a href="/blog/how-to-host-a-white-elephant-gift-exchange/">white elephant gift exchange guide</a> covers the mechanics.
- **A quick holiday trivia round.** Ten questions, teams of 2-3, done in 15 minutes — enough to fill a lull without taking over the night. See our <a href="/blog/how-to-host-a-trivia-night/">trivia night guide</a> if you want to build out a longer round.
- **Mingling games between contest rounds.** A deck of conversation prompts or a quick icebreaker keeps the room moving instead of clumping into the same three conversations all night.

## 5. A few things that flatten most ugly sweater parties

- **No real contest structure.** "Everyone just wears a sweater" is fine for a work happy hour, but a party without an actual judged moment loses its centerpiece — people came dressed up for a reason, give it a payoff.
- **One winner-take-all category.** It turns a silly, inclusive bit into a single popularity contest, and everyone who didn't win feels like the sweater didn't matter.
- **No numbering or tracking system for votes.** Without it, judging turns into an argument about who wore what, right when you want the room's attention on the reveal.
- **Treating the sweaters as the whole party.** Once the contest wraps, the room needs something else to do — don't let the energy crater just because the main event is over.
- **Forgetting non-competitors.** Not everyone wants to enter, and that's fine — make sure there's a reason to be there beyond watching other people get judged, whether that's food, music, or a side game running in another corner.
